SlopGene

A free, open-source and scriptable plasmid and sequence editor for Windows, macOS and Linux.

  • Windowssetup program, .exe
  • macOSdisk image, .dmg
  • LinuxDebian and Ubuntu, .deb
  • Linuxother distributions, AppImage

Each download installs the desktop app and the slopgene command line.

The installers are not code-signed, so Windows and macOS warn before the first start, and the README says which button to press. Older versions, the plain archives and the checksums are on the releases page.

See it work

Cutting GFP out of one plasmid and putting it into another. Click the gene on the map, press Clone, and the protocol opens as a bench. A click on a card zooms into it, where the enzymes that cut nearest the part are picked, and the join shows the new plasmid with its bases underneath.

Copying a part out by PCR instead, when no enzyme cuts near it. The primers are designed for the part, and the enzymes chosen go on their tails, so the copy has the same sticky ends as the opened vector.

What it does

  • Opens and saves .dna, GenBank and FASTA files, and draws circular and linear maps.
  • Edits sequences, features and primers, with undo, and finds the sites of 581 commercial enzymes.
  • Designs primers and simulates PCR, digests, restriction cloning, Gibson assembly and Golden Gate.
  • Builds a cloning construct step by step on a bench, with every junction checked.
  • Aligns sequencing reads to a map and shows every change, down to its effect on the protein.
  • Does all of it from the command line and from scripts too, with JSON output for pipelines and AI agents.
slopgene sites pUC19.dna --unique
slopgene digest pUC19.dna -e EcoRI,PvuII --gel gel.png
slopgene verify "sequencing/*.gbk" --refs maps/
slopgene excise donor.dna --feature GFP --vector pUC19.dna --json

The README on GitHub has the full list of functions, the keyboard shortcuts, the scripting guide and the source code.
